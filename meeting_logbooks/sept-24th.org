* September 24th Log

** Simulation Bugs Found and Fixed

The refactor reorganized the repo (designs in ~designs/<bird>/<name>/~, one agent guide in ~AGENTS.md~,
project skills). More importantly it added a *golden harness* that records every output the repo produces.
A pure cleanup has to reproduce those outputs exactly, so any number that moved pointed at a real change.
That is how most of these bugs surfaced.

*** Harmonic Clock Bug: Why Harmonics overfit dt
- Forgot to reset ~data.time~ after the 2000-step wing-pitch settle, so each harmonic started with a phase
  offset of k * omega * 2000 * dt. That offset depends on dt.
- Same on MJX. A harmonic tuned at dt 0.06 was being played back at a different phase than dt 0.03, which
  is exactly what looked like harmonics being specific to one discretization.
- Related: CPU replay ran the harmonics on a slightly different clock from the MJX optimizer whenever the
  crank speed was tuned.
- *Fix:* reset the clock after the settle, use one shared clock definition, stamp new artifacts with their
  clock, and add a flag (~design_io --pre-clock-fix-dt~) to rotate old ones.
- *Takeaway:* harmonic designs now give the same result across dt. The earlier conclusion that harmonics
  don't transfer is withdrawn.

*** Aero Bugs
The blade-element aero overpredicted lift by ≈75%. Several coding mistakes all pushed the same way:
- *Lift sign errors.* Lift direction, center of pressure and rotational lift all assumed the hinge edge
  always leads. The far edge leads for ≈40-50% of the stroke, and during that part lift pointed the wrong way.
- *Aero doesn't match the wing mesh.* The aero put the hinge on the leading edge and started the strips at
  the hinge; the real mesh has its leading edge 17 mm ahead of the hinge and its root 6.7 mm out. The aero
  now takes its geometry from the mesh.
- *Rotational terms were wrong.* Rotational lift was scaled by the strip's radius instead of its width;
  rotational drag was a crude sum that never sampled the leading edge.
- *Result:* the seed design's lift dropped from ≈362 mN to ≈187-210 mN, and its certified peak torque rose
  from 174 to 193 mNm. The co-designed designs still beat the seed by ≈12%, so co-design still pays; the
  absolute numbers were wrong. Every lift or torque figure from before Sept 24 is stale.
- *New behavior revealed:* the passive wing pitch is *bistable*. It settles into a ≈190 mN or a ≈210 mN
  mode depending on dt and integrator, so designs must be compared per mode.

*** CPU and MJX simulators didn't do the same thing
MJX is only useful if it matches the CPU reference. Both did not do the same thing:
- The CPU computed aero on the previous step's wing pose.
- Between aero updates (every 4 steps), the CPU re-applied the old force at the wing's new position, while
  MJX held it fixed.
- The two runner scripts defaulted to different designs and updated aero at different rates.
- The MJX gradients had a NaN at zero wing velocity.
They now agree almost completely: ≈0.02% on lift and peak torque.

*** The optimizer measured and replayed the wrong things
- The evolutionary search ranked designs with a smooth estimate of peak torque that runs 20-30% low and
  reordered half of them. It now ranks on the exact per-cycle peak.
- A lift-steadiness penalty was measured over the wrong period, which quietly stopped the search from
  changing crank speed.
- Several replay and warm-start paths rebuilt a different design from the one that was trained (wing-locked
  runs, seed files decoded by position, the GUI's GA -> Adam hand-off).
- A timestep check could pass without testing anything, and training stages overwrote each other's
  checkpoints.
- *Takeaway:* what the optimizer reports now matches what CPU replay shows.

*** Silent input errors and GUI issues
- *.tg parser:* a typo'd key silently fell back to its default (~timstep~ -> 0.375 ms). Angle units were
  guessed, so ~pitch: 2 deg~ became 2 radians. Unknown keys now raise, and units come from the literal.
- *GUI:* one plot was always blank; tendon tension was measured from the wrong rest length; the learned
  stroke plane applied in only one tab; the plot cache could serve stale results; Stop didn't stop a run;
  the Viewer tab couldn't launch.

*** Left unfixed on purpose
Listed in ~AGENTS.md~ "Known bugs", because each fix changes recorded outputs: e.g. the 300 mN
~--lift-floor~ default is now unreachable, and the motor-tendon ranges are L/R asymmetric.


** Free-flight hover of the standalone bird

Goal: stable hover of the free-standing bird at real gravity, with nothing holding it up except its own
flapping.

*** 1) Making the bird physically honest
- Pillars don't count as weight; each crank disk is 2.5 g. The bird is 23 g = 226 mN.
- Fake forces to simulate hover removed (the stabilizing body wrench, the render assists), reduced gravity
  removed.
- Hover-friendly physical properties: level stroke plane, lighter tendon damping and no pitch armature, so
  the passive pitch is periodic.
- Body inertia spread out (same mass). Without it the body recoiled against every wingbeat and lost all its
  lift: -15 mN free vs 239 mN held.
- Key finding: The bird must hover pitched ≈21 degrees.

*** 2) The foundation: drive, state, launch
- *Controls:* a crank speed plus 3 Fourier harmonics per crank for all four cranks (25 numbers). Different
  waveforms on left/right and front/back cranks produce roll, pitch and yaw moments. That is the only way
  the bird can steer.
- *Phase-locked cranks:* the plain speed servo let the crank phases drift apart by a fraction of a degree
  per wingbeat, which slowly changed the wing motion and pushed the bird out of trim. Locking each crank to
  its commanded angle (like an encoder) fixed it. Before the lock, candidates that looked stable still fell.
- *Once-per-wingbeat state:* the policies see 12 numbers once per wingbeat: position, attitude, velocity,
  rotation rate. The timestep is set so a wingbeat is a whole number of steps, so the state is always
  sampled at the same point in the stroke.
- *Launch cradle:* releasing from rest while the wings spin up tumbles the bird. A cradle holds only its
  /average/ pose while the flapping settles, then lets go, so at release the bird is already on its natural
  flapping cycle.

*** 3) Open Loop Policy
- *Trim:* whatever the cradle is still pushing when it lets go is exactly what the open loop fails to
  supply. A Newton solver adjusts the harmonics and tilt until the cradle carries nothing. Result: 8.54 Hz,
  harmonic depths of ≈7% at most.
- *Trimmed is not stable:* flown with no feedback, the bird tips over after 40-50 wingbeats (≈5 s). The
  linear model explains it: a small error grows ≈7.6% per wingbeat. This is a property of flappers, not a
  bad waveform; open loop cannot hold hover.
- *Search:* CMA-ES (evolutionary, no gradients) explored only waveform changes that keep the bird trimmed,
  scoring how long and how level it flies. Best: ≈49 wingbeats vs 38-47 for the trim across timesteps. A
  real but small gain, which confirms the ceiling.

*** 4) Closed Loop Policy
- *Linear model:* perturb each state and each harmonic slightly, fly one wingbeat, measure the response.
  This gives a per-wingbeat linear model of the bird.
- *LQR:* the optimal linear controller on that model. It picks harmonic corrections for the next wingbeat
  from the current state; errors shrink by at least 34% per wingbeat.
- *MLP:* the LQR is distilled into the MLP (12 -> 64 -> 64 -> 24) by training it to copy the LQR's
  outputs. It corrects the Fourier waveform once per wingbeat, bounded to ±15% depth.
- *Result:* hovers 14 s+ with < 0.4° tilt and ≈1 cm drift. Recovers from pushes up to 0.8 m/s and 4 rad/s
  roll/pitch.

*** 5) What diffsim did and didn't do
- Training through MJX over several wingbeats diverged, both from scratch and starting from the LQR. The
  bird is unstable and the tendon/contact dynamics are stiff, so gradients over long horizons blow up.
- Gradients over /one/ wingbeat are accurate and match the finite differences. Diffsim could build the
  linear model, but the shipped one came from CPU finite differences.
- *Takeaway:* for this bird, linearize one wingbeat and use classical control. Long-horizon training through
  diffsim doesn't work here.

*** 6) Where it lives
- ~designs/hummer/free-hover/control.tg~ holds both policies; ~control_open_loop.tg~ is the CMA-ES open loop.
- To fly: pick one in the GUI's Viewer tab (P pushes the bird, O toggles the closed loop), or
  ~mjpython src/view_free_hover.py~.
