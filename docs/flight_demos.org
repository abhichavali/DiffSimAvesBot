* Overview

Five interactive MuJoCo simulations of the free-flying tensegrity flapping-wing
robot (design ~designs/hummer/hover~), all launched from one
viewer via a ~--mode~ flag:

#+begin_src bash
mjpython src/view_hover.py                    # hover      (default)
mjpython src/view_hover.py --mode vector      # force vectoring + aero-force arrows
mjpython src/view_hover.py --mode multimodal  # up/down-stroke asymmetry + aero arrows
mjpython src/view_hover.py --mode flying      # visual hummingbird over the mechanism
mjpython src/view_hover.py --mode collision   # obstacle brought into a wing, then removed
#+end_src

Every mode sits on top of the *same stabilized hover*: the flapping carries the
weight and a feedback controller keeps the body upright and on-station. So the
document starts with how hovering was built (the hard part), then the shared
build pipeline, then each mode.

| Mode       | What you see                                                        |
|------------+---------------------------------------------------------------------|
| hover      | Upright, on-station, holds altitude, recovers from nudges           |
| vector     | Wing motion scripted beat-by-beat (tilt, stop, resume); force arrows |
| multimodal | Precessing stroke plane -> up/down-stroke asymmetry; force arrows    |
| flying     | Semi-transparent hummingbird skin over the tensegrity joint + motors |
| collision  | A red pole enters a wing's arc, is hit, then removed; contacts shown |

Key files:
- ~opt/hover_policy.py~ -- free-flight model build and the (differentiable) trainer.
- ~src/runtime/hover_control.py~ -- the controller: ~level_stroke_plane~,
  ~pd_init_theta~, the features and the stabilizing wrench.
- ~src/view_hover.py~ -- the mode-based viewer (controller + per-mode schedules
  + live aero arrows).
- ~src/flying.py~ -- post-compile XML surgery (~to_airframe~,
  ~shrink_pillar_visual~, ~enable_wing_collision~, ~add_mocap_obstacle~,
  ~add_visual_bird~).
- ~designs/hummer/hover/hover_policy/~ -- the saved controller (~policy_best.npz~) + ~hover_meta.json~.

* How the hover was built (the foundation)

This was the hard part; everything else is a layer on it.

** The problem: the mechanism is fundamentally unstable in free hover

The grounded design is a bench rig -- the pillars are welded to the world. To
fly it, ~flying.to_airframe~ reparents every world-welded body into a single
~airframe~ body on a ~<freejoint>~ (mobile struts keep their own freejoints;
pillar mass is lightened to 10 g each). Total flying mass ~73 g.

Measured open-loop (no control), with gravity matched to the flap lift, the free
body *tumbles to 90-180 deg within ~15 wingbeats at every timestep tried
(0.13-0.40 ms)*. No timestep is passively stable -- Euler numerical damping only
delays the tumble. A cycle-averaged linear policy over crank-speed / stroke-plane
authority cannot catch it (confirmed in MJX too).

The physical reason: real hovering insects and hummingbirds stabilize by
modulating each wingbeat (within-wingbeat control). These wings are quasi-static,
so the flapping just makes lift plus disturbances with no fast control handle.

*Conclusion:* stop asking the flapping to stabilize the body. Let the flapping
supply the /lift/, and add a separate controller that supplies the /flight-control
authority the mechanism lacks/.

** The approach: gravity-matched lift + a 6-DOF stabilizing wrench

1. *Match gravity to the mean flap lift* so hover is even possible (real weight
   >> lift). The mean lift is measured on the grounded bench; gravity is set so
   ~weight = lift~ (~385 mN -> ~5.27 m/s^2 for the leveled config).
2. *Level the stroke plane* (~level_stroke_plane~: set the crank-disk mount pitch
   from the design's 20 deg to 0). The 20 deg pitch pumped a constant /forward
   thrust/ that dragged the bird off-station; leveling makes the flap a pure
   vertical stroke (and actually raises lift, 363 -> 385 mN).
3. *The controller applies a 6-DOF wrench to the airframe body*: a world-frame
   force (3) + a body-frame torque (3), written into
   ~data.qfrc_applied[dofadr:dofadr+6]~ of the airframe freejoint each step.
   (A tested MuJoCo quirk: a freejoint's translational ~qfrc_applied~ is
   body-frame, torque body-frame -- convenient, since the flap forces are also
   body-frame.)

This is non-physical (a virtual "flight controller"), which is fine here --
transfer past the visualizer was explicitly not required.

** The controller: a feature-normalized diagonal PD

The state fed to the controller (~hover_features~) is, for the airframe freejoint:

: [ pos_err(3), linvel(3), att_err(3), angvel(3) ]      # 12 features

- ~pos_err~   = airframe position - hover set-point
- ~att_err~   = small-angle rotation vector vs upright (2 * quat.xyz)
- these are *EMA low-pass filtered* (removes the fast flap "bob" so the gains see
  slow drift) and *feature-normalized* by ~FEATURE_SCALE = [20,20,20, 10,10,10,
  5,5,5, 2,2,2]~ so a "typical" deviation is ~O(1) and the gains it needs are
  ~O(1) instead of ~80x too small in raw metres.

The control law (~policy_commands~, ~pd_init_theta~) is a diagonal PD:

: force  = -kp_pos * pos_err - kd_pos * linvel     (tanh-bounded by f_max)
: torque = -kp_att * att_err - kd_att * angvel     (tanh-bounded by tau_max)

*The key insight -- attitude authority is the lever.* Attitude and position are
coupled through the lift vector: with weak attitude gains the body settles at a
~56 deg lean, which vectors its lift sideways (~0.32 N horizontal); that
saturates the position force and it drifts a metre. Crank the attitude gains up
and it stays within ~4 deg upright, the lift stays vertical, and position holds
within centimetres. Fixing attitude fixes position.

Winning gains (found by a gain sweep in the simulator):

| gain     | value | axis                    |
|----------+-------+-------------------------|
| kp_pos   |   6.0 | position -> force        |
| kd_pos   |   2.5 | velocity -> force        |
| kp_att   |   0.4 | attitude -> torque       |
| kd_att   |   0.1 | angular rate -> torque   |
| f_max    |   0.4 | per-axis force bound [N] |
| tau_max  |  0.25 | per-axis torque bound [Nm] |

Result: *tilt <= 4.3 deg, horizontal drift <= 40 mm, altitude locked, and it
recovers from velocity kicks* (vs open-loop tumbling to 180 deg and drifting
~1 m).

** Why it is a sim-tuned PD, not a gradient-trained policy (honest note)

The full plan was to /train/ the controller with the differentiable simulator.
~opt/hover_policy.py~ builds the entire differentiable MJX rollout (JAX
blade-element aero + wing-pitch + the wrench) and does reverse-mode BPTT
(~jax.checkpoint~ remat + ~opt.iterations=1~ to make it fit and be
differentiable).

It does not work: *the gradients explode (~1e11) through this stiff, unstable
flapping rollout regardless of horizon or starting point* -- even starting from
the working PD, gradient descent walked /away/ from it. So gradient training
cannot produce or improve a stabilizer here.

What works is the *structured PD above, whose handful of gains were tuned by a
sweep in the (differentiable) simulator*. The differentiable sim was used heavily
to build and evaluate everything; gradient descent specifically was the one tool
that did not pan out. (The controller was developed on a vast.ai RTX 5070 Ti,
since destroyed; the result is committed in ~designs/hummer/hover/hover_policy/~.)

* The shared build pipeline (all five modes)

Every mode builds the model the same way, then layers its own behavior:

#+begin_src text
compile_tg(tg)                     -> grounded bench scene.xml
flying.to_airframe(xml)            -> reparent welded bodies into one free airframe
flying.shrink_pillar_visual(xml)   -> trim the dangling pillar stilts (cosmetic)
[mode-specific XML: collision / flying transforms]
MjModel.from_xml + Euler + gravity + dt (from hover_meta.json)
level_stroke_plane(model)          -> pure vertical stroke
raise all free bodies + set-point by --hover-height (ground clearance)
#+end_src

Per step (~step_forces~ in ~view_hover.py~):
1. mode schedule (crank factor / stroke-plane tilt / obstacle position),
2. ~hover_features~ -> EMA -> ~policy_commands~ (the PD) -> wrench into
   ~qfrc_applied~,
3. aero via ~AeroBladeRuntime~ (CPU quasi-steady blade-element),
   wing pitch via ~WingPitchLimiter~,
4. constant crank drive ~a0 * crank_factor~ on the four motor actuators.

*Ground clearance:* the assembly spawns sitting on the floor, so ~--hover-height~
(default 0.25 m) lifts /all/ free bodies together (tendons don't stretch) and
raises the controller set-point to match -- it then holds ~0.1-0.2 m of clearance
instead of standing on the ground.

*Live aero-force arrows* (~draw_aero_arrows~): each frame, per-blade thin arrows
plus a bold net-force arrow per wing are pushed into ~viewer.user_scn~ via
~mjv_initGeom~ / ~mjv_connector~ (blue = left wing, orange = right). Forces come
from the aero runtime's cached per-strip forces/points, in world frame -- so the
arrows point wherever the aero force actually points.

* Simulation 1: hover (default)

*What it shows.* The bird flaps and hovers in place, upright, holding altitude
above the floor; nudge it (it recovers). This is the bare foundation with no
extra schedule.

*How it works / was built.* Exactly the shared pipeline above: constant crank
drive (~crank_factor = 1~), leveled stroke plane, the PD wrench holding
position + attitude. Measured: tilt ~4 deg, drift ~27 mm.

#+begin_src bash
mjpython src/view_hover.py
mjpython src/view_hover.py --openloop   # same rig, controller off -> it tumbles
#+end_src

* Simulation 2: vector (force vectoring)

*What it shows.* /Force vectoring/ = steering the /direction/ of the aerodynamic
force by changing how the wings move (not just its magnitude). Over an 8 s cycle
(~--period~) the wing motion is scripted beat-by-beat: level flap -> tilt the
stroke plane forward -> tilt aft -> slow to a *full stop* -> resume. Live
aero-force arrows swing with the stroke-plane tilt and collapse to nothing when
the wings stop.

*How it works / was built.*
- ~vector_schedule(t, period)~ returns ~(crank_factor, sp_tilt, sp_pitch)~ for the
  current phase. ~crank_factor~ multiplies the flap drive ~a0~ (down to 0 =
  wings stopped); ~sp_tilt/sp_pitch~ are applied through
  ~DiskOrientation.set(...)~, which rewrites the crank-disk mount quaternions so
  the whole flap arc -- and hence the mean force -- tilts.
- The aero arrows (drawn into ~viewer.user_scn~) are computed from the CPU aero
  runtime's cached per-strip forces, so they track the vectoring and vanish at
  the stop.
- The controller's force bound is raised (~f_max = 0.9~) so the body stays a
  steady platform even when lift briefly drops to zero -- you watch the /force/
  change, not the body flailing.

Verified headless: min crank factor 0.00 (wings fully stop) and net aero lift
~0 mN at the stop.

#+begin_src bash
mjpython src/view_hover.py --mode vector
mjpython src/view_hover.py --mode vector --force-scale 2.5 --period 6
#+end_src

* Simulation 3: multimodal (up/down-stroke asymmetry)

*What it shows.* Multi-modal flight: the up-stroke and down-stroke are not
symmetric, so the aerodynamic force is stronger on the power stroke. The aero
arrows pulse asymmetrically through the cycle.

*How it works / was built.*
- ~multimodal_sp(t, period)~ /precesses/ the stroke-plane normal -- it returns a
  ~(tilt, pitch)~ that sweeps a slow circle (~sin~ and ~sin+pi/2~), applied each
  step via ~DiskOrientation.set(...)~. The wing therefore spends part of the
  cycle on a forward-tilted (asymmetric) plane and part on a symmetric one, so
  the per-stroke aero load swells and shrinks.
- The wing-pitch dead-zone (~WingPitchLimiter~) already feathers the wing
  differently up vs down; the precession makes that asymmetry visible in the arrows.
- Flap drive stays full; the PD wrench holds the hover (~f_max = 0.6~).

#+begin_src bash
mjpython src/view_hover.py --mode multimodal
#+end_src

* Simulation 4: flying (visual body over the mechanism)

*What it shows.* The tensegrity joint, wings, and motors mounted on a /visual
body frame/ -- a semi-transparent rufous-hummingbird skin -- with the whole robot
flying/hovering. You see the mechanism through the translucent body.

*How it works / was built.*
- ~flying.add_visual_bird~ adds textured body/head/tail meshes (parented to the
  ~airframe~ body, so they fly with it) at ~20% opacity, and swaps the two
  placeholder wing geoms for the real left/right rufous wing meshes. The meshes
  are not in this repo: they are referenced in place from the sibling
  ~Tensegrity-Playground~ checkout when it exists, else from ~BIRD_ASSETS_DIR~
  or ~--bird-assets~ (one of which is then required).
- *Mass fix (important):* the mesh geoms otherwise inherit MuJoCo's default
  density and the skin weighs ~2.3 kg -- 32x the 73 g bird -- so it dropped like
  a rock. The skin is purely cosmetic, so ~visual_torso~ is given an explicit
  tiny inertial (~mass = 1e-6~); MuJoCo then ignores the mesh-derived mass.
  Skinned mass is back to 73.07 g (identical to bare), weight = lift, and it
  hovers. (The wings already had explicit inertials, so swapping their meshes
  changed nothing.)
- Same PD hover controller underneath.

#+begin_src bash
mjpython src/view_hover.py --mode flying
#+end_src

* Simulation 5: collision (obstacle in, then out)

*What it shows.* From a stable hover, a red pole is brought into a wing's arc,
the wing hits it (the bird staggers), then the pole is removed and the controller
recovers the hover. The collision mesh is on and MuJoCo's contact points/forces
are drawn.

*How it works / was built.*
- ~flying.enable_wing_collision~ gives each wing a thin invisible collision
  capsule along its span; the visible wing mesh stays collision-free (a mesh
  collider would force MJX onto its slow convex path). Two build subtleties, both
  of which broke the demo until fixed:
  - *The capsule must be massless* (~density="0"~). A solid capsule at default
    density is ~26 g /per wing/ -- it pushed the 73 g bird to 475 g and it sank
    like a stone. (Same class of bug as the visual skin.)
  - *Asymmetric collision masks* so the wing hits ONLY the obstacle: wing =
    ~contype 2 / conaffinity 4~, obstacle = ~contype 4 / conaffinity 2~. A
    symmetric bit made the two wing capsules collide with /each other/ at the top
    of the stroke (and with the struts, bit 1) and blow up the sim on start.
- ~flying.add_mocap_obstacle~ adds the pole as a *mocap body* -- kinematic,
  infinite mass, its pose driven by ~data.mocap_pos~ each frame -- so it can be
  teleported in and out while still colliding (a welded body could not be moved
  at runtime), and its own mass never weighs on the bird.
- ~collision_obstacle(t, period, park, hit)~ parks the pole far away, moves it
  *into the wing's arc* (offset ~dy~0.04~, deep enough that the fast-moving wing
  whacks it firmly -- ~14 simultaneous contacts) from 12% to 52% of the cycle
  (enters early), then parks it again. Smaller ~--obstacle-dy~ / fatter
  ~--obstacle-r~ = harder hit.
- The contact viz flags (~mjVIS_CONTACTPOINT~, ~mjVIS_CONTACTFORCE~) are enabled
  on the viewer; the PD wrench (boosted to ~f_max 0.6~ here) fights the
  perturbation and re-settles.

Verified headless: parked = 0 contacts and a clean hover; during the obstacle-in
window the wing clips the pole (tilt bumps to ~7-10 deg) and recovers, altitude
held.

#+begin_src bash
mjpython src/view_hover.py --mode collision
mjpython src/view_hover.py --mode collision --obstacle-dy 0.11 --obstacle-r 0.04
#+end_src

| ~--obstacle-dx/-dy/-dz/-r~ | Reposition/resize the collision pole               |
| ~--openloop~      | Turn the controller off (watch it tumble)                   |

* Summary

- *Hover* is the load-bearing piece: the flapper cannot self-stabilize, so the
  flapping carries the (gravity-matched, stroke-plane-leveled) lift and a
  feature-normalized diagonal-PD wrench -- tuned by a simulator gain sweep, with
  attitude authority as the key lever -- holds it upright and on-station.
- The other four modes are thin schedules layered on that hover: scripted wing
  motion + force arrows (*vector*), a precessing stroke plane + force arrows
  (*multimodal*), a massless cosmetic skin (*flying*), and a movable mocap
  obstacle against a collidable wing (*collision*).
